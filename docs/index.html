01 — The loop

One decision,
four hundred times

Formula Runner is not a driving simulator and does not pretend to be. It is a single repeated choice — which gap, how close — taken at increasing speed until you get it wrong.

01

Auto-accelerate

The throttle is not your problem. The car pulls toward its top speed on its own, and steering stays fully independent of it. Braking exists. You will rarely want it.

02

Thread the gap

Traffic arrives in authored patterns that always leave a lane open. Slide past with under 2.4 m of clearance and it registers as a near miss — the combo climbs and holds for 2.6 seconds, up to ×8.

03

Spend the boost

Each near miss charges the bar — 13% at ×1, closer to 20% at ×8. Cash it in for 26% more speed, which shortens every gap ahead and raises what each one pays. The risk compounds on purpose.

  • Scoring Distance scaled by speed. A near miss pays 120 × combo, tightened by how close you cut it (×0.8 to ×1.4); an overtake pays 60 × (0.7 + 0.3 × combo) — 186 at ×8, not 480.
  • Run length Minutes, not hours. One collision ends it, and one click puts you back on the road.
  • Difficulty Spawns tighten from 1.14 s to 0.68 s and traffic speeds up from 92 to 198 kph over the first 5,200 m.
  • Your data A versioned local save holds your best score, garage, and credits. Nothing leaves the browser.
  • Garage Engine, Tires, Turbo, and Aero — 12 ranks each. One strong run cannot finish two trees. After a crash you can double credits once; a break plays every six crashes, never mid-race.
  • Original No licensed teams, circuits, liveries, or audio. Every car and marking is built in-engine.

04 — Run it yourself

Clone,
install, race

Vite, TypeScript, and Three.js. No engine to download, no editor to open, no account to make.

terminal
$ git clone https://github.com/McSupplyCo/Formula-Runner-2.0.git
$ cd Formula-Runner-2.0
$ npm install
$ npm run dev

# then open the local URL and click Race
# checks: npm test · npm run typecheck · npm run build
